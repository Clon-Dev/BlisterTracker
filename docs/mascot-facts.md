- `cycle-hormones`: In the first half of the cycle oestrogen rises and an egg matures. After ovulation, progesterone prepares the womb lining. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/periods/fertility-in-the-menstrual-cycle/))

- `missed-periods`: Missed 3 periods in a row and the pregnancy test is negative? That's worth a doctor's visit. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/periods/))

## The pill

- `pill-hormones`: The combined pill contains two hormones, oestrogen and progestogen, and stops the ovaries from releasing an egg. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/combined-pill/what-is-it/))

- `pill-effective`: Taken correctly, the pill is over 99% effective. Missed pills make it less effective. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/combined-pill/what-is-it/))
- `pill-perfect-vs-typical`: With perfect use, 3 to 10 in 1,000 pill users get pregnant in a year. In everyday life it's 24 to 70. That's why taking it on time matters. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungsmethoden/pille/))
- `pill-break-protected`: If you've taken your pills correctly, you're still protected during the 7-day break. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungsmethoden/pille/))
- `pill-break-bleeding`: With the pill, you usually bleed like a period during the 7-day break or while taking the dummy pills. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/combined-pill/how-to-take-it/))

## Minipill

- `minipill-breastfeeding`: The minipill can be taken while breastfeeding. It doesn't reduce the amount of breast milk. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungsmethoden/minipille/))

- `pop-no-break`: A mini pill pack has 28 pills and you take no break between packs. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/progestogen-only-pill/how-to-take-it/))

## Other contraception

- `condom-effective`: Condoms are up to 98% effective when used correctly every time, and 82% with everyday use. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/condoms/))
- `condom-sti`: Condoms are the only method that protects against both pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections, including HIV. ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/family-planning-contraception))
- `condom-lube`: Never use oil-based lubricant with latex condoms. Water- or silicone-based lubricant is safe. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/condoms/))
- `condom-double`: Two condoms at once aren't safer. They can rub and split. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/condoms/))
- `condom-expiry`: Check a condom's use-by date and the CE mark. An expired condom may not work. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/condoms/))
- `implant-rod`: The implant is a 4 cm rod under the skin of the arm. It's over 99% effective for 5 years. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-implant/what-is-it/))
- `implant-removal`: After the implant is removed, the chance of getting pregnant returns to what it was before, straight away. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-implant/what-is-it/))
- `iud-copper`: The copper coil (IUD) contains no hormones. It's over 99% effective and lasts 5 or 10 years, depending on the type. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/iud-coil/what-is-it/))
- `ius-hormonal`: The hormonal coil (IUS) lasts 3 to 8 years and can make periods lighter, shorter and less painful. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/ius-hormonal-coil/what-is-it/))
- `larc-effective`: Implants, coils and sterilisation are highly effective and long-lasting, because there's nothing to remember. ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/family-planning-contraception))
- `fertility-awareness-less`: Fertility awareness methods and withdrawal are less reliable than modern contraception. ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/family-planning-contraception))
- `global-use`: Worldwide, 874 million women use modern contraception, and 164 million would like to but can't. ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/family-planning-contraception))
- `patch-size`: The contraceptive patch is a 5 cm square you wear on your skin. It releases oestrogen and progestogen into the blood. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-patch/what-is-it/))
- `patch-schedule`: Patch: wear one for 7 days, then swap it. After 3 patches comes a patch-free week, with a possible period-like bleed. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-patch/how-to-use-it/))
- `patch-placement`: Stick the patch on clean, dry, hairless skin that won't rub on tight clothes: upper arm, back, bottom or tummy. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-patch/how-to-use-it/))
- `patch-effective`: The patch is over 99% effective when used correctly. With mistakes it's 91%, so about 9 in 100 women get pregnant in a year. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-patch/how-to-use-it/))
- `patch-start`: Start the patch in the first 5 days of your period and you're protected straight away. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-patch/how-to-use-it/))
- `patch-loose`: Patch came loose or you forgot to swap it? What to do depends on where you are in the cycle. Ask a pharmacist, doctor or nurse. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-patch/how-to-use-it/))
- `patch-fertility-return`: After you stop the patch, your fertility usually returns to previous levels after around 1 to 3 months. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-patch/what-is-it/))
- `ring-size`: The vaginal ring is soft plastic, about 5 cm across. It releases oestrogen and progestogen and stops ovulation. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/vaginal-ring/what-is-it/))
- `ring-schedule`: The ring stays in for 21 days, then out for 7 days before a new one goes in. You may have a period-like bleed in that break. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/vaginal-ring/how-to-use-it/))
- `ring-feel`: Putting the ring in is similar to inserting a tampon. Once it's in place you shouldn't feel it, not even during sex. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/vaginal-ring/how-to-use-it/))
- `ring-disposal`: Put a used ring in its pouch and throw it in the bin. Never flush it down the toilet. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/vaginal-ring/how-to-use-it/))
- `ring-effective`: The vaginal ring is over 99% effective when used correctly. If not used correctly, it's 91%. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/vaginal-ring/how-to-use-it/))
- `ring-fertility-return`: After you stop the vaginal ring, your fertility usually returns to previous levels after around 1 to 3 months. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/vaginal-ring/what-is-it/))
- `patch-ring-medicines`: St John's wort and some medicines for TB, epilepsy or HIV can stop the patch or ring from working. Ask a pharmacist. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/vaginal-ring/how-to-use-it/))
- `injection-duration`: One contraceptive injection lasts 8 to 13 weeks, depending on the type. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/contraceptive-injection/what-is-it/))

## Emergency contraception

- `ec-sooner`: Emergency contraception works better the sooner it's used. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/emergency-contraception/))
- `ec-pills-window`: Levonorgestrel pills work up to 3 days (72 hours) after sex, ulipristal pills up to 5 days (120 hours). ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/emergency-contraception/))
- `ec-iud`: The copper coil is the most effective emergency contraception. It can be fitted up to 5 days after sex. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/emergency-contraception/))
- `ec-germany-pharmacy`: In Germany, the morning-after pill is sold in pharmacies without a prescription. Under 22 with statutory insurance, a prescription beforehand makes it free. - `ec-pill-interaction`: The combined pill can affect how some emergency contraception works, so tell the pharmacist you take it. ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/contraception/methods-of-contraception/combined-pill/missed-or-extra-pill/))
- `ec-breastfeeding-de`: After the morning-after pill with levonorgestrel, pause breastfeeding for at least 8 hours. After ulipristal, pause for at least a week. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungsthemen/verhuetung-nach-der-geburt/))
- `ec-timing-de`: The morning-after pill works up to 5 days after unprotected sex, but best within 12 hours. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungspannen/pille-danach/))
- `ec-mechanism-de`: The morning-after pill delays or prevents ovulation. It's not an abortion pill and probably doesn't stop a fertilised egg from implanting. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungspannen/pille-danach/))
- `ec-side-effects-de`: After the morning-after pill, headache, nausea, stomach ache, spotting and tender breasts are possible. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungspannen/pille-danach/))
- `ec-pregnancy-test-de`: After the morning-after pill, take a pregnancy test at the latest when your period is more than a week late. ([familienplanung.de](https://www.familienplanung.de/verhuetung/verhuetungspannen/pille-danach/))
